02The problem · why they called

They didn't need a chatbot. They had one, and it generated noise

The institution already had a website chatbot: a menu-driven Einstein Bot. That was the problem. Prospects had to navigate fixed options instead of simply asking what they wanted to know, so many dropped off before reaching an answer. The leads that did come through were often incomplete or unqualified, leaving the admissions team to triage rather than advise.

After · ask in your own words (illustrative)
Can I do the data science degree part-time while working?
Yes. The part-time track runs in the evenings, and the next intake opens soon. Want the fees and entry requirements?

With answers spread across a large knowledge base and a separate program catalog, the bot was also slow to respond, compounding the drop-off. They needed it to actually generate pipeline.

03Why it mattered · the stakes

A bot that can't answer plainly quietly caps growth

Every drop-off is a prospective student who went to ask a competitor instead.

For an institution recruiting at this scale, the website is the first and often only touch a prospect has before deciding where to inquire. Every unqualified lead is advisor time spent triaging rather than converting the ones who are ready. A bot that can't answer plainly isn't a neutral cost; it caps growth at the widest part of the funnel.

04Our approach · why it worked, not just what we built

We measured first, then rebuilt the flow around what the data showed

The gains didn't come from a bigger model.

  1. Measured before we optimizedWe turned on agent analytics and traced every response from query to answer, and found that ~80% of prospect queries could be answered from the knowledge base alone. That finding reshaped the whole flow.
  2. Made the knowledge base the source of truthThe agent checks knowledge first and only falls back to a live web crawl for program and fee data, cutting redundant calls and sharpening response time.
  3. Added friction where it paysA short pre-chat qualification form filters out spam and half-formed inquiries, so the leads that reach admissions are real and ready to advise.
  4. Put the numbers in the business's handsEvery metric is reconciled against agreed definitions and published as native Salesforce reports, so stakeholders track the agent's impact themselves.
05What we built · delivered

An agent that answers, captures and reports

Conversational agent

Natural-language Q&A across 1,000+ published knowledge articles. Prospects ask in plain words and get an answer, not a menu.

Automated lead capture

A pre-chat form with email validation and consent, writing qualified leads straight into Salesforce with their interests attached.

Knowledge-first retrieval

Answers ~80% of queries directly from the knowledge base, with a web-crawl fallback for live program and fee details.

Business-ready reporting

Salesforce-native reports on leads, conversion, escalation and CSAT, in marketing and admissions' own dashboards.

06What we learned · if you're doing this too

Three things that decide whether this works

  • Instrument before you optimizeThe 80%-answerable finding reshaped the whole design. Without analytics on first, we'd have tuned the wrong things.
  • Let prospects ask in their own wordsA conversational agent self-qualifies where a menu just filters: the same traffic yields more, and better, leads.
  • Friction in the right place raises qualityA short qualification step up front cut junk without shrinking the funnel. Volume more than doubled and conversion held.
07Partnership · what the institution owned

They own the knowledge and the scoreboard; we built the agent on top

The institution owned what only it could: the knowledge, the live program and fee data, and the definitions behind every metric. That last part is where most "impact" claims fall apart, so we reconciled every number against definitions the team agreed to and published them as native Salesforce reports the institution runs itself.
